Quantum P1NG (Q-P1NG®) is engineered from the ground up on the principle of Zero-Telemetry and Absolute Data Minimization. As a commercial security suite, our business model relies strictly on selling software licenses, never on monetizing, tracking, profiling, or aggregating user data.

1. Who Is Responsible

Q-P1NG® is published by ZonkeyNet (Quantum P1NG), the controller of the limited personal data described below. For any privacy question or request write to our legal team (show email).

2. What We Collect

  • No Account, No Personal Identifiers: Q-P1NG needs no account. We do not require, collect, or store your phone number, email address, legal name, contacts, or location.
  • No Telemetry or Tracking SDKs: The app contains no analytics, no advertising frameworks, no crash reporting services and no background telemetry.
  • Pseudonymous Keys: Your identity in Q-P1NG is a set of keys created on your phone. Contacts, and the relays that carry Q-FEED posts and offline messages, see the public keys involved, never your name or phone number.
  • Local Cryptographic Keys: All cryptographic keys (the RSA-2048 identity key in the Android Keystore, AES-256-GCM message keys, X25519/Ed25519 keys for the mesh and radio links) are generated locally on your hardware. Private keys never leave your device.
  • What Reaches Us: only what you choose to send us (for example a support email, a Q-FEED report sent by email or a report about AI content sent from the app, see section 5) and, if you buy Q-P1NG Pro, the order details described in section 9.

3. Message Handling & Zero-Knowledge Architecture

  • End-to-End Cryptography: Every communication is encrypted on the sender's device and can only be decrypted by the intended recipient.
  • Zero-Knowledge: We have no mathematical or architectural capability to decrypt, inspect, or reconstruct your messages, voice notes, or file attachments.
  • Direct Delivery, No Message Servers: Messages travel directly between phones over Tor onion services, the Bluetooth/Wi-Fi mesh or LoRa radio. Q-P1NG runs no server that stores or routes your messages.
  • Offline Mailboxes: When a trusted contact is offline, an encrypted envelope can wait on our own Nostr relay, an onion service reached only through Tor, or, if it cannot be reached, on public Nostr relays run by third parties, also through Tor. The relays cannot read it, but they see the sender's and recipient's public keys, the time and the size. Our relay keeps no logs, accepts only Q-P1NG mailbox envelopes and Q-CAST station data, and deletes envelopes after 7 days and station data after 30; third-party relays keep what they receive according to their own policies.

4. Anonymity & Network Connectivity

  • Native Tor Integration: Q-P1NG® natively supports connections through the Tor network to obscure geographical location and physical network origins. Services reached through Tor see a Tor exit address, not your IP address; services reached directly see your IP address.
  • Onion Routing: Users can communicate exclusively via .onion hidden service addresses, completely bypassing standard internet gateways.
  • Our Online Services: Q-P1NG operates a few small services on Cloudflare Workers, none of which keeps request logs: the optional temporary email tool, reached through Tor by default, which holds a temporary address and the messages it receives until it expires (24 hours by default, at most 7 days) or you delete it; the report service for AI content (section 5); the licence service for purchases on our website (section 9); and the news feed, when available, always reached through Tor, which receives only the language and topics requested. To use the temporary email tool your installation registers once with a random token after a short computation on the phone (a proof of work), with no account or device identifier; only the phone that created a temporary address can open its mailbox, and at most 3 addresses can be created every 24 hours. Cloudflare processes these requests as our hosting provider.
  • Calls and Secure Live: call and live audio and video never pass through our servers: they travel directly between phones or, over Tor, through a relay on the called contact's phone. Only for fast (non-Tor) connections, the phone asks our STUN server for its own public address; that server sees the address and port of the request at that moment, stores nothing and keeps no logs. The same server hosts the Nostr relay described in section 3.
  • Offline Map Downloads: Only when you tap Download, example maps come from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com, files published in the Quantum-P1NG repository), or from a GitLab mirror (gitlab.com) if GitHub cannot be reached, and map areas you choose are extracted from Protomaps' public builds (build.protomaps.com), through Tor when Tor is active. These hosts see a Tor exit address or, in direct mode, your IP address, and can tell which area is requested. Example maps are installed only if their size and SHA-256 fingerprint match the values built into the app, or those of the map catalog that Q-P1NG signs and publishes on GitHub, whose signature the app checks. The online map view loads tiles from OpenStreetMap and OpenFreeMap servers.

5. AI Features

  • On-Device by Default: Q-Brain text models, Whisper dictation and Q-Dream image generation run on your phone with models you download. Prompts, recordings and images handled by on-device models never leave the phone.
  • Model Downloads: Models come from Hugging Face and from catalogs published by Q-P1NG on GitHub, through Tor unless you choose a direct download. These services see which files are requested.
  • Optional Online Providers: Only if you add an online AI provider yourself (for example OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Mistral, Groq, DeepSeek, xAI, OpenRouter, or your own server) with your own API key, the prompts and conversation context you send to it go from your phone directly to that provider (through Tor for .onion endpoints). The provider sees your IP address and that content and handles them under its own privacy policy and your account terms. Your API keys are stored encrypted on your phone.
  • Reporting AI Content: every answer and generated image has a Report action. A report contains the category you pick, an optional note, the app version and edition and, only if you tick the box, the reported answer or image prompt. It is sent through Tor when Tor is active, stored by our report service for one year and emailed to our team for review; no account, IP address or device identifier is attached.
  • Web Search: When you or the assistant search the web, the query goes to DuckDuckGo or Brave Search, through Tor and their onion services by default.

6. Q-Globe (In-App Globe, Radio & Live TV)

  • Isolated Viewer: Q-Globe opens the public page globe.qp1ng.com in a separate process with its own browsing storage. It receives no account, contact, chat identity or device identifier, sets no cookies and contains no analytics. Its browsing data is erased when Q-Globe closes. To open faster, it keeps a copy of the globe's public files (the page, places and stations, the same for every user, at most 24 MB) and checks them for updates at every opening; the layers you open, map tiles, searches and playback are not kept. The copy shows that Q-Globe was used, not what you looked at, and the panic wipe erases it.
  • Network Mode: Q-Globe follows the app's connection mode. In Tor mode its requests travel through Tor and never fall back to a direct connection; in direct mode they use your normal internet connection.
  • Third-Party Services: The globe shows public data and imagery from third-party sources, and radio stations and TV channels are streamed from their broadcasters. These services receive ordinary network requests: through Tor they see a Tor exit address, in direct mode they see your IP address. Q-P1NG asks before any playback, warns before a direct stream during a Tor session, and does not control the logging policies of these services.
  • Sharing: A place, event or station is shared only when you choose to, either through Q-P1NG's encrypted chat or with another app you select. Only the public globe link is shared.

7. Q-SWEEP (Counter-Surveillance & Tracker Guard)

  • On-Device Sensors: The magnetic probe, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi scans and the local network check run on your phone. Their results stay in the current session unless you export a summary yourself. The local network check runs only after you confirm it and only inside your own Wi-Fi network.
  • Background Tracker Guard (optional, off by default): When you turn it on, Q-SWEEP listens for a few seconds about every 15 minutes for Bluetooth trackers that announce they are away from their owner. Only when such a tracker is nearby does it read your phone's location, to tell places apart. This is why Android asks for location “Allow all the time”.
  • Stored Only on Your Phone: Sightings are kept encrypted on your device. Bluetooth addresses are replaced by a keyed hash, positions are deleted after 24 hours, and alerts (counts and times, no places) after 7 days. Nothing is transmitted to us or to anyone else. Turning the guard off deletes the positions; the panic wipe erases everything.
  • Play Sound: Only when you tap it, Q-P1NG connects to that one tracker to send the standard sound command its maker provides for people who find it. Nothing is read from the tracker or stored.

8. Other Services the App Contacts

These requests happen only when you use the feature, through Tor when Tor is running and otherwise with a direct connection, except where marked Tor only. Each service receives an ordinary network request and handles it under its own policy.

  • Q-FEED: posts you make public, and your public reactions and reports, are published to public Nostr relays and can be read by anyone; the app reaches relays through Tor when you prefer Tor.
  • Radio Information Commands: weather (Open-Meteo), Wikipedia, earthquakes (USGS), disasters (GDACS), space weather (NOAA SWPC), security advisories (CISA) and BBC news feeds.
  • Satellites: orbital data from CelesTrak and SatNOGS and the ISS position from Open Notify.
  • Bitcoin Prices: CoinGecko, Kraken, Binance or CryptoCompare. Transaction links open mempool.space in your browser only when you tap them.
  • Linux Shell: packages come from the Alpine Linux mirror (dl-cdn.alpinelinux.org) always with a direct connection, only when you install or update the shell or its packages.
  • Automations and Link Previews: automation templates from GitHub and previews of links in your chats (Tor only).
  • Tor Status: check.torproject.org and public IP echo services (ipify, icanhazip, ifconfig.me, ipapi.co, ipwho.is), to show the Tor exit address (Tor only).

9. Purchases and Updates

If you buy Q-P1NG Pro through Google Play, Google processes the payment under its own privacy policy; we never see your card or bank details. Google shares with us the order details needed to provide and support the purchase (order number, product, date, country and refund status). The app checks your purchase with Google Play on your phone. We keep order records as long as tax and accounting law requires.

  • Bitcoin Payments on Our Website: you pay to a new address of our wallet; no account, name or email is asked. Our licence service keeps the order (address, amount, euro price and exchange rate) and the country your connection comes from, which tax law requires as evidence for VAT, and asks mempool.space whether the address has received the payment. Bitcoin transactions are public on the blockchain. Orders are deleted 90 days after payment, abandoned ones after about two weeks; we keep the sale record (date, amount and country) as long as tax law requires.
  • Licence Keys: the key is checked on your phone against a public key built into the app. The app never contacts us to validate it, and the key contains only a random number, the edition and the date of issue.
  • Updates of the Website Edition: the edition downloaded from our website checks once a day, if you leave the option on, for a small update file that Q-P1NG signs and publishes on GitHub, and downloads updates from GitHub only when you tap Download, through Tor when Tor is active. GitHub sees a Tor exit address or your IP address. An update is installed only if its size, SHA-256 fingerprint and signing certificate match. The Google Play edition is updated by Google Play.
